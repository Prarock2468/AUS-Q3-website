SharePoint Intranet vs. Microsoft Viva vs. a Custom Portal: An Honest Comparison

Deepak Mishra Technology Leader

Quick Summary

Choosing between SharePoint, Microsoft Viva, and a custom portal depends on your budget, desired control, and speed. SharePoint offers a familiar starting point, Viva enhances engagement within Microsoft 365, while custom portals provide maximum flexibility.

Every internal comms lead, IT director, and HR head has had this conversation at least once: “Our intranet feels dead. Nobody uses it. What do we replace it with?” The honest answer is not simple, and anyone who tells you it is probably has something to sell you. The real debate today usually comes down to three paths: stick with a classic intranet built on SharePoint, layer Microsoft Viva on top of what you already own, or walk away from both and build something custom. This is not a theoretical debate about SharePoint vs Microsoft viva alone — it is a decision that shapes how 500, 5,000, or 50,000 employees start their workday, find information, and feel connected to the company they work for. We have built all three types of solutions for our clients, so this comparison is not drawn from a datasheet. It is drawn from what actually happens after go-live, when the excitement of a new intranet meets the reality of daily use.

What is the Difference Between SharePoint and Microsoft Viva?

SharePoint has been around since 2001 and built its reputation as the backbone of enterprise content management — the place where documents live, teams share files, and intranets are built page by page. Microsoft Viva, launched in 2021, is a very different kind of platform. Viva is Microsoft’s employee experience platform, a family of modules — Viva Engage, Viva Connections, Viva Insights, Viva Goals, and Viva Learning — that sit on top of Microsoft 365 and Teams and focus on how people feel about work.

  • SharePoint: Document libraries, site pages, permissions, workflows, and enterprise search — built for structure and content.
  • Viva: Pulse surveys, wellbeing nudges, learning paths, goal tracking, and recognition feeds — built for people and culture.
  • The overlap: Viva Connections actually runs on top of a SharePoint home site, so most companies end up running both together rather than choosing one over the other.

Is Microsoft Viva Included in Microsoft 365?

Partially, and this is where a lot of budgets go sideways. Some Viva modules — Viva Connections, Viva Engage, and a basic version of Viva Insights — are bundled into most Microsoft 365 and Teams licenses at no extra cost. The heavier modules, including Viva Goals, Viva Learning, and the premium tier of Viva Insights, need a separate Viva Suite license that is billed as an add-on per user, per month, stacked on top of whichever Microsoft 365 collaboration tools license you already pay for. This structure trips up a lot of finance and procurement teams, because “Viva” sounds like one single product on an invoice, when in reality the true cost depends entirely on which modules HR and IT decide to switch on for the whole company.

Should I Use SharePoint or Build a Custom Intranet?

This is the question that opens almost every project we scope at Q3 Technologies, and there is no universal right answer — but there is a reliable way to think it through.

  • Choose SharePoint when speed matters more than uniqueness. A well-configured SharePoint intranet portal can go live in a matter of weeks, works natively inside the Microsoft ecosystem your teams already use, and covers most of what a typical company needs: company news, document libraries, staff directories, and basic branding.
  • Choose a custom build when your workflows do not fit a template. Regulated industries, multi-brand organizations, and companies with layered approval chains often outgrow SharePoint’s out-of-the-box structure within a year or two of launch.
  • Choose a hybrid when you want both worlds. Many of our clients start with SharePoint as the foundation and layer custom intranet portal development on top — branded dashboards, custom web parts, and integrations with SAP, Workday, or ServiceNow — getting Microsoft’s stability alongside the flexibility of custom code.
  • Do not decide on cost alone. A cheap SharePoint rollout that nobody adopts ends up costing more, in wasted licenses and lost productivity, than a well-scoped custom project that people actually use every day.

Which is Better for Employee Engagement, SharePoint or Viva?

The statistics here are worth sitting with. Recent Gartner engagement research found that only 31% of employees say they feel genuinely engaged and energized by their work, and close to 70% report they do not feel the connection to their job that they should (source below). That gap is not strictly a SharePoint problem or a Viva problem — it is a digital workplace design problem, and the two tools attack it from different angles. SharePoint drives engagement indirectly, by making information easy to find and cutting down the daily friction that quietly wears people out. Viva drives engagement directly, through recognition feeds, pulse surveys, wellbeing check-ins, and manager-level insights.

Separate research shared by Powell Software found that employees report being over 70% more engaged and efficient when their intranet is genuinely personalized to their role, instead of a generic, one-size-fits-all page (source below). In practice, this makes the real comparison less about Microsoft Viva vs SharePoint as rivals fighting for the same job, and more about how thoughtfully either one is configured and personalized for your specific workforce. A poorly built SharePoint site loses to a well-built Viva rollout every time and the reverse is just as true.

How Much Does a Custom Intranet Portal Cost vs SharePoint?

Numbers vary with scope, industry, and how many integrations you need, but here is the honest range we see across real projects:

  • Out-of-the-box SharePoint: often close to “free” if you already hold Microsoft 365 licenses, but budget for setup, branding, and governance — typically $10,000 to $40,000 for a mid-size rollout when it is done through proper SharePoint development.
  • Customized SharePoint intranet: $40,000 to $150,000 or more, depending on custom web parts, integrations, and multilingual needs — this is where experienced Microsoft development services partners earn their fee by avoiding expensive rework later.
  • Fully custom intranet portal: $80,000 to $300,000 or more, built on modern frameworks and hosted on Azure, with no ceiling on design or feature complexity — squarely the territory of specialized custom software development services providers.
  • Ongoing cost: custom portals usually carry a higher year-one cost but a lower long-term licensing bill, since you are not paying a per-seat add-on every time you want to switch on a new capability.

So, Which One Should You Actually Choose?

There is no single universal winner in the SharePoint-versus-Viva-versus-custom debate, and anyone who claims otherwise is oversimplifying a genuinely layered decision. Start with what your employees actually struggle with today — finding documents, feeling disconnected, or fighting a rigid system — and let that pain point guide you toward the right mix. Most enterprises we work with do not end up picking just one option; they build a foundation with SharePoint, add Viva where employee experience data matters most, and bring in custom development wherever their workflows are too specific for an out-of-the-box template. If you are still weighing your options, a conversation with a team that has actually built all three is worth more than another feature comparison chart. FAQs

1. Can SharePoint intranets be integrated with Microsoft 365 Copilot and AI agents?

Yes. Modern SharePoint environments can use AI-powered agents to help employees find information, answer questions, and interact with content based on their existing permissions. Q3 Technologies can help organizations design AI-enabled intranets that connect enterprise knowledge with intelligent search and workflows.

2. How can Q3 Technologies customize Microsoft Viva Connections for enterprise use?

Q3 Technologies can extend Viva Connections using SharePoint Framework (SPFx), Adaptive Card Extensions (ACEs), custom web parts, and role-based dashboards. These capabilities allow organizations to surface personalized applications, information, and business tools within Teams and Viva.

3. Can a custom portal integrate SharePoint with SAP, Workday, or ServiceNow?

Yes. A custom intranet can integrate SharePoint with enterprise systems through APIs, connectors, middleware, and custom applications. This enables employees to access business information and initiate workflows from a unified digital workplace rather than switching between multiple systems.

4. Is SharePoint Framework (SPFx) still relevant for modern intranet development?

Yes. Microsoft continues to position SPFx as the recommended customization and extensibility model for SharePoint Online, with support extending across Microsoft Teams and Viva Connections. It can also support modern technologies such as React, TypeScript, and Microsoft 365 integrations.

5. How can organizations personalize a SharePoint or Viva intranet for different employee roles?

Organizations can create role-based dashboards, audience targeting, personalized navigation, targeted news, and custom web parts so employees see information and applications relevant to their responsibilities. Viva Connections supports personalized experiences and audience-specific dashboards across desktop and mobile.

6. When should an enterprise choose a custom portal instead of SharePoint or Viva?

A custom portal is generally more suitable when an organization requires complex workflows, advanced integrations, highly specific business logic, multi-brand experiences, or capabilities beyond standard Microsoft 365 functionality. Q3 Technologies can also take a hybrid approach, combining SharePoint and Viva with custom development to balance enterprise security, scalability, and flexibility.
